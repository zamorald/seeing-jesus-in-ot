Patterns of Christ in the Story of Joseph

Sent By The Father

Genesis 37:2, 13–14

…Joseph, being seventeen years old, was pasturing the flock with his brothers. He was a boy with the sons of…his father’s wives. And Joseph brought a bad report of them to their father….
And Israel said to Joseph, Are not your brothers pasturing the flock at Shechem? Come, I will send you to them. And he said to him, Here I am. So he said to him, Go now, see if it is well with your brothers and with the flock, and bring me word.

Jacob not only counts his son Joseph a trustworthy messenger, but he entrusts Joseph with assessing the status both of his brothers and of his father’s flocks.

As a trustworthy messenger, Joseph is duty-bound to give his father the truth about his brothers’ behavior in the field and the welfare of the animals in their care.

In the same way, Jesus, duty-bound to the truth, was sent by the Father to see how His brothers (the leaders of Israel) and His Father’s flock (the people) were doing:

John 5:22-23, 30

“The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, that all may honor the Son, just as they honor the Father. Whoever does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him….
I can do nothing on my own. As I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just, because I seek not my own will but the will of him who sent me.”

Just as Joseph was ready and eager to accomplish whatever his father asked of Him, Jesus also sought not His own will, but the will of His Father who sent Him.

John 8:42-47

Jesus said to them, If God were your Father, you would love me, for I came from God and I am here. I came not of my own accord, but he sent me. Why do you not understand what I say? It is because you cannot bear to hear my word. You are of your father the devil, and your will is to do your father's desires…. But because I tell the truth, you do not believe me. Which one of you convicts me of sin? If I tell the truth, why do you not believe me? Whoever is of God hears the words of God. The reason why you do not hear them is that you are not of God.

No matter how truthful it was, Joseph’s report—as Jesus’s—likely displeased the others.

His Father’s Favoritism Made His Brothers Jealous

Genesis 37:3-4

Now Israel loved Joseph more than any other of his sons, because he was the son of his old age. And he made him a robe of many colors. But when his brothers saw that their father loved him more than all his brothers, they hated him and could not speak peacefully to him.

The pattern here: God’s chosen servant often garners the ire of the existing authorities when he comes with authority from heaven to do God’s will.

This pattern is clearly at play when Daniel comes to power in Babylon:

Daniel 6:3-4

Then this Daniel became distinguished above all the other high officials and satraps, because an excellent spirit was in him. And the king planned to set him over the whole kingdom. Then the high officials and the satraps sought to find a ground for complaint against Daniel….

Though Daniel in fact is a faithful servant both of God and the king of Babylon, the high officials did not want to see him promoted to second in command—above themselves—or his Jewish brothers put into positions of power. (Joseph, of course, will be promoted to second in command by Pharaoh, and ultimately the Father puts all things beneath Jesus’s feet.)

In the gospels, the shrewd Roman governor Pilate was able to discern that the leaders in Jerusalem had betrayed Jesus because they were envious of Him:

Mark 15:10

[Pilate] perceived it was out of envy that the chief priests had delivered [Jesus] up.
