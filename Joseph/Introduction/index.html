Why This Study?

Is There Something New Here?

Someone once said, If it’s true, it’s not new; if it’s new, it’s not true. And there is something to that saying. The truth of God stands through the ages unchanged, as it is written, There is nothing new under the sun (Ecclesiastes 1:9). The saying can serve as a memorable warning to reject innovations in religion, that is, practices and teachings that are new in the sense that they are foreign to Scripture.

However, too many have used this slogan to dismiss any possibility of learning something new from the Bible. What a lost opportunity! We ought always to be growing and abounding in the Lord, as it is written, If these qualities are yours and are increasing, they keep you from being ineffective or unfruitful in the knowledge of our Lord Jesus Christ (2 Peter 1:8). Or do we need to be reminded, Though by this time you ought to be teachers, you need someone to teach you again the basic principles of the oracles of God (Hebrews 5:12)? Why would we be content to come so far and go no further?

Well, some have said, That’s not how they taught it when I was growing up. If your parents were Christians, that’s good! But if they were faithful Christians, they themselves told you that they were not the standard of truth—the Bible is! If they missed something in Scripture, they don’t want you to miss it, too, just for the sake of some supposed family solidarity; they want you to do better than they did—all parents want this for their children. No, any thought that, That’s not how I learned it, only serves to establish Christians as the standard of truth rather than the Bible; this cannot be the way.

Haven’t we overlooked the rest of the passage in Ecclesiastes that we first mentioned above?

Ecclesiastes 1:9–11

What has been is what will be,
    and what has been done is what will be done,
    and there is nothing new under the sun.
Is there a thing of which it is said,
    “See, this is new”?
It has been already
    in the ages before us.
There is no remembrance of former things,
    nor will there be any remembrance
of later things yet to be
    among those who come after.

What we think of as new is perhaps only forgotten—It has been already in the ages before us. There is no remembrance of former things….

And it isn’t wise, either, to think once we have learned something it will be around forevermore from this day forward, since …Nor will there be any remembrance of later things yet to be among those who come after.

The point is this. The word of God is indeed the truth and the only truth—and it’s not new, so nothing true can be new. But something true can be new to us. The saying, If it’s true, it’s not new; if it’s new, it’s not true, can only be absolute if we already know absolutely everything there is to know about God’s word.

Our Responsibility

God’s word is an investment: He gave it to us so we could take and use it to bring Him a return. If we refuse to grow and increase in knowledge, we take our place with the one-talent man who in fear buried his master’s money instead of using it (Matthew 25:14–30)!

Whatever was written in former days was written for our instruction, that through endurance and through the encouragement of the Scriptures we might have hope (Romans 15:4). The Spirit says the Old Testament is important for giving us endurance, encouragement, and hope. Are we resisting the Spirit by not reading it?

How is our reading of the Old Testament going? Doesn’t it make sense to look for any methods or approaches the writers of the Bible used when they interpreted Moses and the prophets? How did the Gospel writers employ prophesy? How did the apostles support their teaching with Scripture in an age before the completion of the New Testament?

Did Jesus say anything to the disciples about reading the Old Testament? Of all people the churches of Christ ought to know the answers to these questions!

A New Ancient Reading

After His resurrection, Jesus directly instructed the apostles how to interpret the Old Testament. What did He tell them, and how do we know? Answering these questions is the goal of this entire study.

Luke 24:27

Beginning with Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things concerning himself.

Thus, in ancient times, when Christ was yet on earth with His disciples, He put forth a method of interpreting the Old Testament. The method is what the Spirit had been using all along when writing the Old Testament, but it was when Christ was on the road to Emmaus that He brought it to our attention.

1 Corinthians 2:8–10

None of the rulers of this age understood this, for if they had, they would not have crucified the Lord of glory. But, as it is written,
    “What no eye has seen, nor ear heard,
        nor the heart of man imagined,
    what God has prepared for those who love him”—
these things God has revealed to us through the Spirit….

The apostles, for their part, preached the gospel to the entire world starting from the reading Jesus showed them. Peter’s sermon in Acts 2:14–36 features extensive readings in the prophet Joel and more than one Psalm of David, as well as other allusions to Old Testament writings.

Not only did the apostles harness the power of Jesus’s teaching, but those who heard them also proved powerful in the work of the Lord by using the Old Testament:

Acts 6:10

They could not withstand the wisdom and the Spirit with which [Stephen] was speaking.

Acts 18:27–28

When [Apollos] arrived, he greatly helped those who through grace had believed, for he powerfully refuted the Jews in public, showing by the Scriptures that the Christ was Jesus.

At first, the apostles had the advantage of having been with Jesus and having heard explicit details from Him on how to use the Old Testament. But today they have no advantage over us, because they wrote everything down and collected it into a single volume: the Bible! When we read their writings, we also have the interpretations Jesus gave the apostles on the road to Emmaus. And they are just as binding as any other New Testament pattern.

Ephesians 3:3–4

…The mystery was made known to me by revelation, as I have written briefly. When you read this, you can perceive my insight into the mystery of Christ….

2 Peter 1:13–15; 3:1–2

I think it right, as long as I am in this body, to stir you up by way of reminder, since I know that the putting off of my body will be soon, as our Lord Jesus Christ made clear to me. And I will make every effort so that after my departure you may be able at any time to recall these things.

This is now the second letter that I am writing to you, beloved. In both of them I am stirring up your sincere mind by way of reminder, that you should remember the predictions of the holy prophets and the commandment of the Lord and Savior through your apostles….
